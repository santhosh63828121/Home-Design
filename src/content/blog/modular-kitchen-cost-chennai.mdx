## What actually drives the cost

Kitchen pricing comes down to four things, roughly in order of impact:

1. **Size and layout.** A compact L-shaped kitchen uses far less material than a U-shaped or island kitchen. More running feet of cabinetry means more carcass, more shutters and more hardware.
2. **Finish.** The shutter finish is the biggest material lever. Laminate is the most cost-effective; acrylic and membrane sit in the middle; PU and veneer are premium. We cover this in detail in our [laminate vs. acrylic guide](/blog/laminate-vs-acrylic-finishes).
3. **Hardware.** Soft-close hinges, tandem drawers, tall-unit pull-outs and corner solutions add convenience — and cost. Branded hardware lasts longer and is worth it on the units you use daily.
4. **Accessories and appliances.** Chimney, hob, sink, faucet and countertop stone are usually quoted separately, because every family wants something different here.

This split is deliberate — it keeps the headline price honest instead of bundling in assumptions about appliances you may already own.

## How to get more kitchen for your budget

A few decisions give you the best return:

- **Spend on the busy zones.** Put premium hardware and finishes where you cook and store daily; save on the loft and rarely-opened units.
- **Choose layout over square footage.** A well-planned L-shaped kitchen often works better than a sprawling one. Good design beats more cabinets.
- **Mix finishes.** A premium finish on the lower units and a simpler one up top can look intentional and trim cost.
- **Plan storage early.** Tall pull-outs and corner units cost more but prevent the "nowhere to put things" regret later.
