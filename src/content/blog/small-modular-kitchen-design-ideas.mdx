Most Chennai apartments don't have a big kitchen — and that's completely fine. A small kitchen that's well planned beats a large one that isn't. The trick is to use every vertical inch, keep the work triangle tight, and choose finishes that make the space feel open rather than boxed in. Here are nine ideas we use again and again.

## 1. Choose an L-shape or single-wall layout

In a compact kitchen, the layout does the heavy lifting. An **L-shaped** kitchen keeps your fridge, sink and hob within a few steps of each other and frees up the rest of the room. For very narrow kitchens, a **single-wall** layout with tall units is often smarter than forcing a parallel design into a tight space.

## 2. Go all the way up with tall units

Stopping cabinets at eye level wastes the most valuable storage in a small kitchen — the top. Floor-to-ceiling tall units add a huge amount of storage in the same footprint and give the kitchen a clean, built-in look.

## 3. Use the corners properly

Dead corners are where small kitchens lose the most space. Carousel units, magic corners and pull-out organisers turn an awkward corner into genuinely usable storage.

## 4. Pick handleless shutters

Handleless (push-to-open or J-groove) shutters give a small kitchen a smooth, uninterrupted surface. With nothing sticking out, the kitchen reads as calmer and visually larger — and there's nothing to catch your clothes on in a tight walkway.

## 5. Let light bounce around

Lighter shutter colours and a glossy or matte-reflective finish bounce daylight around a small kitchen. If you love a darker look, balance it with a light countertop and backsplash so the room doesn't close in.

## 6. Add under-cabinet lighting

A strip of warm LED under the wall units lights your work surface without shadows and makes the whole kitchen feel considered and premium — a small upgrade with a big effect.

## 7. Build in the bin and the bottles

The things that clutter a small kitchen — the dustbin, oil bottles, foil and cling film — all have dedicated pull-outs. Designing them in from the start keeps your limited counter clear.

## 8. Float the loft for appliances

A loft unit above the tall cabinets is the perfect home for things you use rarely — the extra cooker, festival vessels, suitcases of the kitchen world. Keeping them off the counter is what makes a small kitchen liveable.

## 9. Match the finish to how you cook

A high-gloss finish looks stunning but shows fingerprints; a matte or textured laminate hides daily use better.
