A wardrobe is the one piece of bedroom furniture you open every single day, so it's worth getting right. The look matters, but daily livability is decided by three things: door type, internal organisation and the material that survives Chennai's climate. Here's how to think about each.

## Sliding vs hinged doors

This is the first decision, and it comes down to space and feel.

| | Sliding | Hinged |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Floor clearance needed | None — doors slide in place | Needs swing space in front |
| Best for | Tight rooms, foot-of-bed wardrobes | Rooms with space to spare |
| Access | See ~half the wardrobe at a time | Full, open access to everything |
| Look | Sleek, modern, fewer lines | Classic; allows full mirrors |
| Cost | Generally higher (track + mechanism) | Generally more economical |

**Rule of thumb:** if there isn't comfortable clearance to swing a door without hitting the bed, go sliding. Otherwise hinged gives you full access for less.

## The part people forget: the inside

A beautiful wardrobe with a badly planned interior is a daily frustration. Plan the inside around what you actually own:

- **Long-hang** for dresses, kurtas and formal wear.
- **Short-hang (double)** for shirts and folded-over trousers — doubles your hanging capacity.
- **Shelves** for stacks and bags; **drawers** for innerwear and accessories.
- A **locker** for documents and valuables.
- **Loft storage** above for suitcases and seasonal bedding — essential in compact homes.

<Callout>
Count before you plan. Roughly how many garments hang vs fold? Where do saris, bags and accessories go? An interior designed to your real wardrobe contents beats a generic split every time.
</Callout>

## Finishes — and why Chennai's humidity matters

The visible finish is a style choice — laminate for hard-wearing versatility, acrylic or veneer for a premium look. We compare those in detail in our [laminate vs acrylic guide](/blog/laminate-vs-acrylic-finishes).

But in Chennai the more important choice is the **core material**, because humidity is the real enemy of joinery. Moisture-resistant boards (good-quality BWR/BWP-grade plywood or equivalent) resist swelling far better than ordinary board, especially along the floor line and on walls that back onto bathrooms. Spending here is invisible — and exactly where a wardrobe quietly succeeds or fails over ten years.

## Mirrors, lighting and handles

- A **full-length mirror** on a wardrobe door saves buying a separate one and makes a room feel larger.
- **Internal LED strips** that switch on with the door turn a dark cabinet into something genuinely usable.
- **Handleless or slim profiles** read modern; classic handles suit a more traditional bedroom.

## What drives the cost

Wardrobe price scales mostly with **size (running feet × height), door type, core material and internal fit-out**. A floor-to-ceiling sliding wardrobe with a fully organised interior and loft costs more than a basic hinged unit — but it also stores far more and lasts longer. Decide the internals first; they're what you live with.
