"What's your style?" is the first question every designer asks — and the one most people freeze on. The labels get used loosely, so here is a plain-English guide to the three styles Chennai homes most often lean towards, what actually distinguishes them, and why you almost certainly want a blend.

## Modern

Modern is about **clean lines, simple forms and calm**. Think flat-panel shutters, handleless cabinetry, a restrained palette of two or three colours, and surfaces kept deliberately clear. Decoration is minimal; the materials and proportions do the talking.

It suits people who find clutter stressful and want a home that feels uncluttered and easy to maintain. The risk to avoid: "modern" done cheaply can feel cold or bare. Warmth comes back through wood tones, soft lighting and a few well-chosen textures.

## Contemporary

Contemporary means **"of the moment"** — it borrows the clean bones of Modern but adds softness, curves and current trends. Where Modern is strict, Contemporary is relaxed: a fluted panel here, a textured accent wall there, brass or matte-black hardware, layered lighting and a warmer mix of materials.

For most Chennai apartments today, Contemporary is the sweet spot. It looks designed and current without committing to the discipline of pure Modern or the ornament of Traditional.

## Traditional

Traditional leans into **warmth, detail and craft** — richer woods, profiled (not flat) shutters, decorative mouldings, patterned textiles and a fuller, more layered look. In South-Indian homes it often shows up as a dedicated pooja space, carved accents, brass and a love of natural materials.

It suits people who want a home that feels rooted and characterful rather than pared-back. The risk to avoid: too much detail in a small space can feel heavy — Traditional reads best with room to breathe.

## The honest truth: most homes are a blend

Almost no real home is a textbook single style. The best interiors pick a **dominant** style and borrow from the others:

- A Contemporary base with one Traditional feature — a carved pooja unit, a brass-accented foyer.
- A Modern kitchen paired with a warmer, more Traditional living room.
- Traditional warmth in materials, Modern discipline in layout.

<Callout>
Style is a starting point, not a cage. The goal is a home that feels like **you** — the labels just help you and your designer speak the same language quickly.
</Callout>

## How style affects cost

Style mostly changes cost through **detail and finish**, not floor area. Profiled shutters, mouldings and decorative elements take more labour than flat panels; glossy and veneer finishes cost more than laminate. So a heavily detailed Traditional look will generally run higher than a pared-back Modern one for the same room — but the bigger cost levers are still size, material grade and how much storage you build.
